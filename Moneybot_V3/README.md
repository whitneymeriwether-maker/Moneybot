# Moneybot V3 — Phase 2 research prototype

**NOT DEPLOYED.** This folder is a standalone, offline, broker-disconnected paper-research component. It does not change V2 or execute orders.

## Implemented
- Deterministic gate for an AI proposal to buy an opening-range breakout, with evidence and thesis required. The advisory entry gate independently verifies that the candidate's timezone-aware market timestamp is within the policy's freshness window relative to the current UTC clock (allowing up to five seconds of future clock skew), even when the caller reports a fresh data age. This does not authenticate the source or replace execution-time revalidation.
- Initial V3 policy: 1.5% equity risk per trade, 3% of starting equity daily realized-loss stop, 50% total notional exposure, 10% peak-equity drawdown pause, 25% overnight exposure, maximum two trading sessions.
- Separate overnight-hold eligibility gate, and basic closed-trade evaluator for AI vs baseline.
- Historical one-position simulator caps notional exposure at 50% of current paper equity and entries at 10% of observed minute volume by default, in addition to cash and stop-distance risk limits. Position sizing accounts for configured adverse slippage at entry and at the planned stop exit; gaps through the stop and unmodeled execution costs can still exceed the nominal risk budget. It rejects nonpositive stop prices, malformed bar volumes, and never assumes an entry or exit fill on a zero-volume minute. The simulator also blocks new entries after realized same-day paper losses reach 3% of starting capital (configurable), resetting at the next New York calendar day. Separately, a configurable 10% closed-trade peak-equity drawdown pause blocks new entries across subsequent sessions until the simulation is restarted; it does not reset each morning. This is a closed-trade entry halt, **not** a guaranteed loss cap: gaps and existing positions can lose more than the threshold, and the simulator's portfolio peak-drawdown pause only uses closed-trade equity, not intratrade mark-to-market values. The entry participation cap does **not** model partial exit fills, order-book depth, market impact, or full-session liquidity. Opening-range and breakout signals also require traded volume. This is not a live execution guarantee.
- Opening-range signals and direct historical paper fills skip weekends; this is not an exchange-holiday calendar.
- Read-only IEX daily-bar screening sorts API timestamps by actual UTC instants (not offset-dependent ISO strings) and validates the entire returned history for malformed OHLCV or duplicate UTC dates before choosing the latest 20 bars. This reduces false recency and hidden invalid-record errors; IEX volume is still partial-market data, not consolidated liquidity.\n- Historical IEX minute-bar downloads reject timestamps outside the requested UTC window and normalize explicit timezone offsets before chronological sorting. Malformed or duplicate instants cannot replace a previously validated CSV. This validates time bounds, **not** market-data completeness, corporate-action adjustments, exchange holidays, or point-in-time provenance.
- The `run_backtest` pipeline now rejects **interior missing regular-session minute bars** by default (`max_regular_gap_minutes=1`), rather than silently scoring trades over missing data. A larger integer tolerance may be passed explicitly for exploratory sparse-feed analysis, but this does not reconstruct missing prices. An optional strict boundary check (`require_full_regular_sessions=True`) also requires each observed weekday session to contain both 9:30 AM and 3:59 PM New York minute bars. The six-month SPY research runner enables this check by default. It rejects truncated session starts/ends, but does **not** detect missing entire days, exchange holidays, early closes, zero-volume synthetic bars, or corporate actions. Early-close sessions require an exchange calendar before they can be evaluated in strict mode. IEX-only minute histories may legitimately be sparse; failing closed is preferable to reporting unsupported performance.
- Offline automated unit tests: `python -m pytest -q`.

## Still required before autonomous paper operation
1. Connect an AI model using strictly structured output and source-backed, timestamped evidence; don't trust unverified news or social media as facts.
2. Ingest historical point-in-time OHLCV and permitted news; control look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, stock splits, market holidays, and source licensing.
3. Integrate with the persistent Neon paper engine, using atomic account locks and idempotent fills; gate checks must be repeated at execution time.
4. Implement close/hold scheduling, forced liquidation by the second trading session, gap/slippage assumptions, and conservative fail-closed behavior on missing data.
5. Build reproducible walk-forward historical comparisons and shadow paper trials against V2, with realistic costs and uncertainty intervals. This prototype's closed-trade metric is NOT a full backtester.
6. Add alerts, spending controls for AI API usage, and safeguards against prompt injection in retrieved source text.

The validator does not guarantee loss limits when gaps, delayed bars, or simulation assumptions occur. It has no broker code or live order path.
